About

I have been building for the web professionally since 2011, and full-time in companies since 2013. I prefer product work over outsourcing: the ideal project is one I use myself — the more expertise a developer has in the product, the deeper the involvement and the better the result.

I became interested in design at the same time as programming and consider the two inseparable; code and API design are design too. Design without data is a waste of time (prototypes and MVPs aside) — sufficient, correct data always suggests the obvious next change in an interface. I would not call myself a specialist in user metrics, but I can spot the obvious things.

Testing should be automated, but I am not a fan of TDD. During development I cover the obviously narrow and critical places; after development, the places where problems actually appeared. A programmer who tries to write entirely without bugs is being overly cautious and spends too much expensive time on rechecking.

I was an organizer of the St. Petersburg frontend community spb.frontend() from its founding and hosted its podcast; these days my involvement is limited to maintaining the community website. I also co-host Ponaehali, a podcast that has nothing to do with programming.

In my free time I snowboard and go karting.

AI agents

My current focus is offloading part of the team's work to AI agents. Our agents successfully write end-to-end Playwright tests, reproduce bugs with our fixtures, and fix bugs. To develop and improve this I built a benchmark around the system: it replays a fixed corpus of real tickets already closed by humans, and a separate AI judge scores how close the agent came to the human solution and the actual code. Everything the agents can do — bug fixes and tests alike — is measured by the same benchmark, which is how we track and compare different versions of agents, plugins, skills and so on.

Alongside this I maintain our team's marketplace of plugins for different agent systems — both our own harness and local ones — kept cross-platform on the open Agent Plugins 1.0.0 standard.

Technologies: React, TypeScript, Node.js, Playwright, AI agents, CI/CD, Agile methodologies.

Mobile applications

I participated in the design and developed two mobile applications. In the first one you can interact with more than 300 currencies and tokens (receive, send, buy, view statistics); it is translated into 14 languages, supports 15 fiat currencies, interacts with native APIs (camera, push notifications, clipboard, in-app browser), communicates with the server over WebSockets, and uses JWT authentication with authorization through the most popular providers (e.g. Facebook and Google). It is built for iOS and Android and available in the App Store and Google Play. The Redux state is persisted in IndexedDB, encrypted with a key kept in the device's secret storage. The second application works with a single currency: from the same sources, 22 separate apps are built with different configs and feature sets, largely repeating the functionality of the main one.

Technologies: Cordova, Webpack, TypeScript, PostCSS, React, Redux, Reselect, react-intl, OneSky, bignumber.js.

Website

Senior Frontend Developer at Uploadcare

August 2016 — February 2018

The company is engaged in file storage, processing and distribution. I designed and developed a new company website. The old one was a Django monolith heavily tied to the backend — frontend fixes couldn't be released without a backend release.
